# Final editorial directive (29 Oct 2026)

## Part 1: premium, publication-ready product narrative

Refine the Accord website content into a premium, publication-ready product narrative. Treat this as the final editorial pass, not another brainstorming round. Aim for the clarity, restraint and consistency of an excellent enterprise product website. Improve the actual copy until it earns that standard.

### Preserve the product identity
Keep the headline: **The private deal room for agent-to-agent commerce.**
Accord connects confidential negotiation, mutually approved terms, agent permissions and controlled payments within one deal room.
Hierarchy: **Accord** (the overall product) · **Sealed Table** (confidential agent negotiation) · **Agreement controls** (approved terms governing supported agent permissions and payment requests) · **Payee** (recipient authorization and destination-change controls) · **Glassbook** (the first business application, focused on liquidity agreements). One coherent product, not unrelated tools.

### Lead with value, then explain the mechanism
Within the first screen a visitor should understand: what Accord is; who it is for; what they can accomplish; what to explore next. Concise hero subheading explaining the commercial workflow. No enclave architecture, verification commands, security caveats or feature inventory in the hero. One primary CTA and one secondary CTA, each with a clear purpose and a real destination. Direct and confident without promising more than the implementation supports.

### Make Glassbook prominent and useful
Introduce Glassbook briefly in or immediately below the hero, then give it a substantive section early. Explain: who uses it; what agreement they manage; which liquidity conditions they define; what evidence Glassbook collects; how that evidence relates to the payment decision; what they can inspect in the demonstration. Connect the complete experience: negotiate through Sealed Table, approve the agreement, evaluate its conditions through Glassbook, authorize payment to the recipient established through Payee. Keep Accord broad enough for future applications while making Glassbook the concrete example.
Respect the current scope: venue-level order-book data does not establish a specific market maker's contribution. Do not claim Glassbook proves the market maker earned a fee. State this limit clearly beside the relevant feature without turning the section into a disclaimer.

### Build a deliberate page narrative
1. Hero and product preview. 2. Glassbook as the first application. 3. Why negotiation, approval and payment belong together. 4. The deal-room workflow. 5. Sealed Table and Payee. 6. A concrete security demonstration. 7. Privacy and control boundaries. 8. Focused FAQ. 9. Closing invitation and footer.
Merge or remove sections that repeat the same point. Target approximately **800–1,100 words of public homepage copy**, excluding the FAQ and supporting pages. Fewer strong sections over a long feature inventory.

### Make the writing professional through precision
Clear, memorable headings with specific explanations. Natural sentences, not continuous three-part slogans. Concrete actions and outcomes over abstract benefits. Short paragraphs without fragmentation. Consistent terminology, capitalization and CTA labels. No unnecessary jargon or invented category names. Avoid "revolutionary", "seamless", "trustless", "military-grade" and similar. No competitor dismissals, judge references, or claims that nobody else has solved the problem. Do not describe the product as premium. Weak wording from earlier briefs may be improved while preserving identity and factual boundaries.

### Keep claims credible
Distinguish confidential negotiation from public settlement. Describe recipient authorization without implying legal business verification. Revocation stops new authorizations rather than recalling signed transfers. Attestation provides evidence about execution; it does not prove fair negotiation, truthful external data or commercial delivery. Do not invent deployment status, customer adoption, savings, audits, endorsements, statistics or live counters. Keep limitations close to the claims they qualify. Detailed technical assumptions go on the security or documentation page.

### Deliver
Final navigation and homepage copy; button labels and supporting text; concise Sealed Table, Payee and Glassbook descriptions; a focused FAQ; footer and metadata; essential empty, pending, refusal and confirmation messages. Separate editorial notes from visitor text. Check the page for repetition, unsupported promises, unclear product relationships and weak transitions. One cohesive final version, then a short list of material improvements and unresolved factual dependencies.

## Part 2: a credible research and industry foundation

Show that Accord addresses documented problems in confidential negotiation, agent authorization, recipient verification and performance-based payment. Connect evidence to specific product decisions. Keep research concise on the homepage; depth on a dedicated research page.

Read the original sources (no snippets, aggregators, AI summaries, or the previous brief's list without verification). Distinguish theoretical paper / experimental result / commercial announcement / expert opinion / implemented standard.

Start with: NDAI Agreements (arXiv 2502.07924); NegotiationArena (arXiv 2402.05863); Google AP2 announcement; a16z crypto "The missing infrastructure for AI agents: 5 ways blockchains can help" (Crowley, Catalini, Hall, Harkavy, Levine, Neville; attribute to the actual authors, no institutional endorsement); Simon Willison "The lethal trifecta" (label the payments application as Accord's design interpretation); OWASP LLM01:2025 (do not claim Accord eliminates prompt injection); FBI IC3 BEC; Pay.UK Confirmation of Payee (distinguish bank-account name matching from Accord's domain-control + destination approval); Kraken order-book vs executions docs (aggregate vs attributable; no integration implied). Do not use manipulation scandals as evidence Glassbook would have prevented them.

Homepage section: heading **The research behind the room.** Intro: "Confidential negotiation, delegated authority and payment verification each have an established body of research and practice. These are the foundations informing Accord's design." Three or four cards, each ~40–60 words: plain-language finding; the person or organization responsible; relevance to Accord; direct link. Paraphrase; a short verified direct quote only when materially stronger. End with **Explore the research →**. Place after the product and Glassbook are understood.

Research page organised by questions: Why keep negotiating mandates confidential? How does an agent demonstrate its authority? What should constrain an agent that follows malicious instructions? How should payment destinations be established and changed? What evidence is sufficient to release a payment? Per source: title, authors, date; type and URL; what it establishes; what it does not; which Accord decision it informs. Keep findings separate from interpretation; include competing approaches and limitations.

People and institutions: include named experts only when their original work directly addresses the issue. No implied review, endorsement, investment, partnership or recommendation. **No portraits, institutional seals or logo walls** that could suggest such a relationship. No headings like "Backed by leading institutions" or "What the experts say about Accord". Verify affiliations as of publication date. Never turn a paraphrase into a quotation.

Deliver: revised homepage with the compact research section; complete research-page copy; a claim-to-source table; a short list of claims removed or qualified. Check every quote, date, statistic and attribution against the original before delivery; omit or flag what cannot be verified.
